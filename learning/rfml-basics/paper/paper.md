## 2. Abstract

Open-set recognition (OSR) asks a classifier to label known classes and to
reject inputs from classes never seen in training. We present a small,
fully controlled synthetic benchmark for OSR in radio signal classification
in which every nuisance factor is explicit: each simulated transmitter has
its own IQ imbalance, Rapp power-amplifier compression, phase-noise level and
DC offset, and the data are split at the transmitter level, so no test
device's impairment signature is seen in training. On four known
modulations (BPSK, QPSK, 8PSK, 16QAM), two held-out unknown classes (64QAM,
4PAM) and two auxiliary training-outlier classes (16PSK, 8PAM), we compare
three standard open-set scores under an identical calibration protocol
(threshold set on known validation data at 95% target acceptance): maximum
softmax probability (MSP), distance to class centroids in embedding space,
and the Objectosphere loss with auxiliary outliers. Across five
pre-registered seeds, the distance score reaches a mean AUROC of
0.779 ± 0.013 while MSP stays at 0.343 ± 0.061, i.e. below chance in every
seed: unknowns that are geometrically close to a known constellation are
*accepted* more confidently by softmax than known classes. Objectosphere
improves unknown rejection over MSP (0.158 ± 0.044 vs 0.034 ± 0.012 at the
same calibration target, 5/5 seeds) but remains far below the distance score
(0.511 ± 0.032). A second pre-registered campaign pairs five post-hoc scores
on one model per seed and dissects that Objectosphere gain: Mahalanobis
scoring *underperforms* plain Euclidean distance (0.747 ± 0.019 vs
0.777 ± 0.015, 0/5 seeds in the predicted direction), energy and max-logit
stay below chance like MSP, and swapping the auxiliary-outlier pool shows
the gain on 4PAM unknowns is similarity transfer: 4PAM rejection is
0.404 under an 8PAM-only outlier pool but 0.002 under a 16PSK-only pool
(5/5 seeds). All code, data generation, pre-registration
and per-seed results are released; a laptop CPU reproduces the campaign in
under an hour. The contribution is a transparent measurement instrument, not
a new method.

## 4. Introduction and Motivation

Deep classifiers for radio signals are usually evaluated closed-set: every
test class was seen in training. Operational radio systems violate this
assumption constantly — new emitters, new waveforms and new interference
types appear, and a deployed monitor must say "unknown" rather than force a
known label. Open-set recognition addresses this, but OSR results on RF data
are hard to interpret for two reasons. First, public RF datasets entangle
many nuisance factors (channel, hardware, capture session), so it is unclear
*what* a rejection score reacts to. Second, device-level hardware
impairments are themselves learnable signatures — the entire field of RF
fingerprinting exploits them — so a frame-level split lets a model pass by
memorizing devices instead of recognizing signal structure.

This paper takes the opposite approach to realism: a deliberately small,
fully synthetic benchmark in which every factor is controlled and published.
Each transmitter draws its own impairment parameters once; splits are at the
transmitter level; the unknown classes never touch training, model selection
or threshold calibration; and the decision threshold is calibrated only on
known validation data at a fixed 95% acceptance target. Within this
instrument we ask a narrow question: how do three standard open-set scores —
MSP, distance-to-centroid, and Objectosphere — behave when the unknown
classes are *structurally related* to the known ones (64QAM vs 16QAM, 4PAM
vs BPSK) and the covariate shift between training and test is exactly the
device impairment distribution?

Contributions:

1. A controlled, open-source OSR benchmark for radio signals with explicit
   per-transmitter impairments, transmitter-level roles
   (train / val-select / val-calib / test), and a pre-registered multi-seed
   analysis plan written before the campaign ran.
2. A paired comparison of six open-set scores (MSP, max-logit, energy,
   Euclidean distance, Mahalanobis, Objectosphere) under one calibration
   protocol — the five post-hoc scores evaluated on the *same* trained
   model per seed — with per-seed values, means and dispersions.
3. Three honestly reported cautionary results: every output-space
   confidence score is *anti-correlated* with unknownness for structurally
   related unknowns (AUROC below 0.5); the benefit of auxiliary outliers is
   almost entirely similarity transfer, measured directly by swapping the
   outlier pool (H7); and the principled Mahalanobis upgrade loses to plain
   Euclidean distance at this scale (H5).
4. A sanity pair of companion tasks (closed-set modulation accuracy, and an
   RF-fingerprinting stage with an ideal-device control at chance) that
   validate the impairment model itself.

## 5. Related Work

Open-set recognition was formalized for visual classifiers by Scheirer et
al. [1] and extended to deep networks via OpenMax [2]; Geng et al. survey the
field [6]. Our benchmark evaluates three representative scores from this
literature: the maximum-softmax-probability baseline of Hendrycks and Gimpel
[3], a feature-space distance score that is a simplified (shared identity
covariance) relative of the Mahalanobis detector of Lee et al. [5], and the
Objectosphere loss of Dhamija et al. [4], which trains against auxiliary
known outliers; AUROC is reported following standard ROC methodology [14].

In the RF domain, deep modulation classification was established on
synthetic and over-the-air datasets by O'Shea et al. [7, 8], who showed that
hardware and channel effects substantially influence performance. Open-set
formulations have been applied to transmitter authorization by Hanna et al.
[11], and Li et al. study open-set modulation recognition with
signal-domain expert features [12]. A complementary line, RF fingerprinting
— e.g. ORACLE [10] — demonstrates that device impairments such as IQ
imbalance and DC offset are themselves learnable signatures; this motivates
our central design choice of transmitter-level splits, so a model cannot
reuse the impairment signature of a training device at test time. Our
impairment chain uses the Rapp solid-state PA model [13], and the embedding
network's residual blocks follow He et al. [9]. We claim no superiority of
any score; the contribution is the controlled instrument and the paired
measurement.

## 6. Research Gap and Contributions

RadioML-style benchmarks [7, 8] fix a global impairment model and split at
the frame level; OSR papers on RF data typically inherit these splits. What
is missing is a benchmark where (a) the shift between train and test devices
is explicit and parametrically published, (b) the unknown classes are chosen
to be geometrically related to known ones, and (c) the calibration protocol
is identical across scores, so differences are attributable to the score and
not to threshold tuning. We fill exactly this gap at small scale; we do not
claim coverage of channel effects (no fading or multipath is simulated) or
of real hardware.

## 7. System Model / Problem Formulation

**Signal model.** Each frame is a complex baseband sequence of N = 512
samples at 8 samples/symbol. Symbols are drawn uniformly from the
constellation of the transmitter's modulation, pulse-shaped by a unit-energy
root-raised-cosine filter (roll-off 0.35, span 6 symbols); a steady-state
window is taken past the filter transient. The impairment chain, in order:
(1) Rapp AM/AM compression y = x / (1 + (|x|/A_sat)^{2p})^{1/(2p)} [13];
(2) IQ imbalance y = μx + ν·conj(x) with μ = (1 + g·e^{−jφ})/2,
ν = (1 − g·e^{+jφ})/2; (3) phase noise as a Gaussian random walk
multiplied in as e^{jθ[n]}; (4) a constant DC offset. The frame then
receives a random carrier-frequency offset f ∼ U(−0.01, 0.01)
cycles/sample with random initial phase, and complex AWGN at a specified
SNR, defined on the impaired, CFO-shifted signal before noise.

**Transmitters.** A transmitter fixes (modulation, g, φ, DC, phase-noise
std, A_sat, p) once: g ∼ U(0.9, 1.1), φ ∼ U(−5°, 5°), DC components
∼ U(−0.05, 0.05), phase-noise std log-uniform in [2·10⁻³, 10⁻²] rad,
A_sat ∼ U(1.0, 1.6), p ∼ U(2, 3). The *ideal* device sets all of these to
their identity values.

**Roles.** Known classes K = {BPSK, QPSK, 8PSK, 16QAM}; unknown test-only
classes U = {64QAM, 4PAM}; auxiliary training outliers A = {16PSK, 8PAM}.
Devices are split into train / val-select / val-calib / test; all frames of
one device stay in one role. A and U are device-disjoint and class-disjoint;
U never influences training, model selection or calibration.

**Task.** Given a test frame, output either one of the four known labels or
"unknown". A score s(x) with larger = more unknown-like is thresholded at τ,
where τ is the smallest value accepting ≥ 95% of known val-calib frames
(accept iff s(x) ≤ τ).

## 8. Proposed Method (evaluated scores)

The embedding network is a three-block 1D CNN (Conv-BN-ReLU-MaxPool, widths
32/64/128, global average pooling) on the 2×512 IQ frame; the same
architecture is used for all variants, trained with Adam, early stopping on
known val-select accuracy, best-state restore. The three variants:

- **CE + MSP** [3]: cross-entropy training on K; s(x) = 1 − max softmax.
- **CE + distance** [5-style]: same trained network; class centroids are the
  mean embeddings of the known *training* frames; s(x) = minimum Euclidean
  distance to any centroid.
- **Objectosphere + MSP** [4]: training set additionally contains A-class
  frames labelled as outliers; the loss pushes known samples above a
  logit-norm margin ξ = 10 and outliers toward small-norm uniform logits
  (λ = 0.1); s(x) = 1 − max softmax.

Campaign 2 extends this set with three further post-hoc scores, all
evaluated on **one** CE-trained model per seed so that score comparisons are
paired at the model level and training variance cancels: **max-logit**
(s = −max logit), **energy** (s = −logsumexp of the logits), and
**Mahalanobis** [5] (s = minimum class-conditional Mahalanobis distance
using a shared within-class covariance fitted on known training embeddings,
shrunk by 0.1 toward the scaled identity). Campaign 2 also trains three
Objectosphere **arms** that differ only in the auxiliary-outlier pool —
full {16PSK, 8PAM}, PSK-only {16PSK}, PAM-only {8PAM} — to measure directly
whether the Objectosphere gain is similarity transfer from 8PAM to the
unknown 4PAM.

Metrics: AUROC with unknown as the positive class (Mann-Whitney, ties at
0.5) [14]; known-acceptance rate (denominator: known test frames); unknown
rejection rate (denominator: unknown test frames); closed-set accuracy on
the accepted known frames. Campaign 2 adds AUROC and rejection per unknown
class, AUROC per SNR value and for two bands (≤ 2 dB, ≥ 10 dB; both knowns
and unknowns conditioned on the band), and the OSCR operating curve [4]
(correct-classification rate of all known test frames versus unknown
false-accept rate as the threshold sweeps; we report its area and the CCR at
10% false accepts). Every rate is reported with its n.

## 9. Experimental Setup

Scale `full`: per known class 40/10/10 train/val/test devices, 20/10/10
frames per device; training SNRs −4…20 dB (step 2); test frames generated
per SNR −6…20 dB for the closed-set stage, and on the training grid for the
open-set stage. 20 auxiliary-outlier and 10 unknown devices per class.
Known test n = 400 frames, unknown test n = 200 frames per seed. Seeds
0–4; each seed redraws devices, frames, noise and initialization through
per-role `numpy.random.SeedSequence` streams, so pool sizes of one role
cannot perturb another role's data. Hypotheses H1–H4 and the support
criterion (predicted sign in ≥ 4 of 5 seeds) were fixed in
`experiments/PREREGISTRATION.md` before campaign 1 ran; H5–H8 were fixed in
`experiments/PREREGISTRATION_V2.md` (committed before any campaign-2 run):
**H5** Mahalanobis > Euclidean distance AUROC on the same model; **H6**
energy > MSP AUROC on the same model; **H7** 4PAM rejection higher under the
PAM-only arm than under the PSK-only arm; **H8** CE+MSP AUROC higher in the
low band (≤ 2 dB) than in the high band (≥ 10 dB). Campaign-2 Objectosphere
arms train at seeds s+101/102/103. Software: Python
3.13, PyTorch 2.14 (CPU, 4 threads), NumPy 2.5. Wall time:
1045 s (campaign 1) on a 4-vCPU container. Companion stages validate the
instrument: closed-set modulation accuracy (chance 0.25) with an
impairment-off ablation, and a 10-device fingerprinting stage (chance 0.10)
whose ideal-device control must sit at chance for the impairment model to be
doing what it claims.

## 10. Results and Discussion

All numbers are mean ± sample standard deviation over seeds 0–4, with
per-seed values in `experiments/campaign/aggregate.json` (campaign 1) and
`experiments/campaign2/aggregate.json` (campaign 2).

### Campaign 1: separately trained variants (H1–H4)

**Open-set scores (H1, H2).**

| Variant | AUROC | Known accept (n=400/seed) | Unknown reject (n=200/seed) | Closed-set acc. on accepted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CE + MSP | 0.343 ± 0.061 | 0.958 ± 0.018 | 0.034 ± 0.012 | 0.768 ± 0.080 |
| CE + distance | 0.779 ± 0.013 | 0.952 ± 0.014 | 0.511 ± 0.032 | 0.806 ± 0.039 |
| Objectosphere + MSP | 0.518 ± 0.030 | 0.937 ± 0.023 | 0.158 ± 0.044 | 0.778 ± 0.060 |

**H1 is supported in 5/5 seeds** (per-seed AUROC difference +0.343 to +0.532,
mean +0.437): with the identical trained network and calibration protocol,
the embedding-distance score separates unknowns while MSP is below chance in
every seed (per-seed range 0.247–0.417). At the 0.95 calibration target,
MSP accepts 96.6% of unknown frames; the distance score rejects 51.1% at the
same known-acceptance level. **H2 is supported in 5/5 seeds** (unknown-
rejection difference +0.085 to +0.215, mean +0.124): training against
auxiliary outliers moves softmax in the right direction, but the effect is
small next to changing the score space, and the pre-registered caveat stands
— part of it may come from 8PAM's similarity to the unknown 4PAM. The
distance score is also the most stable (AUROC sd 0.013 vs 0.061 for MSP).

**Instrument validation (H3, H4).** **H3 is supported in 5/5 seeds**:
fingerprinting accuracy on 10 impaired devices is 0.250 ± 0.039 against an
ideal-device control of 0.098 ± 0.015 (chance 0.10, n = 600/seed); every
control seed lies within 3 binomial standard errors of chance, so the
impairment chain — and nothing else in the pipeline — carries the device
identity. **H4 is NOT supported** (3/5 seeds positive, mean difference
+0.015 ± 0.055): removing impairments did not reliably raise CNN1D
closed-set accuracy (0.770 ± 0.021 ideal vs 0.755 ± 0.041 impaired). Our
pre-registered expectation was wrong: at these impairment magnitudes the
modulation task is dominated by noise and CFO, not by device nuisances. We
report this as an honest negative result rather than adjusting the
criterion.

**Memorisation diagnostic** (Figure 5,
`paper/figures/fig5_modulation.png`). The MLP reaches 0.889 ± 0.138 train
accuracy on seen devices but 0.252 ± 0.008 on unseen test devices — exactly
chance (0.25) at every SNR: under a transmitter-level split, a
capacity-rich flat model memorises training devices and transfers nothing. The CNN's gap is modest
(0.833 ± 0.067 train vs 0.755 ± 0.041 test). A frame-level split would have
hidden this failure entirely, which is the benchmark's core design point.

### Campaign 2: paired scorers on one model (H5, H6)

`experiments/campaign2/aggregate.json`; per-seed values and OSCR areas in

score distributions in Figure 6 (`fig6_hists.png`).

| Variant (one CE model per seed unless noted) | AUROC | Unknown reject | Known accept | OSCR area |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CE+MSP | 0.343 ± 0.061 | 0.034 ± 0.012 | 0.958 ± 0.018 | 0.319 ± 0.061 |
| CE+max-logit | 0.302 ± 0.060 | 0.040 ± 0.024 | 0.948 ± 0.024 | 0.265 ± 0.053 |
| CE+energy | 0.315 ± 0.063 | 0.042 ± 0.016 | 0.947 ± 0.023 | 0.271 ± 0.055 |
| CE+distance | 0.777 ± 0.015 | 0.498 ± 0.038 | 0.955 ± 0.023 | 0.591 ± 0.067 |
| CE+Mahalanobis | 0.747 ± 0.019 | 0.482 ± 0.018 | 0.939 ± 0.024 | 0.570 ± 0.064 |

| Objectosphere, 8PAM-only aux (trained) | 0.591 ± 0.080 | 0.234 ± 0.179 | 0.938 ± 0.025 | 0.490 ± 0.049 |

**H5 is NOT supported** (0/5 seeds, mean difference −0.030): with a shared
within-class covariance shrunk by 0.1 toward the scaled identity,
Mahalanobis scoring is consistently *worse* than plain Euclidean distance on
the same embeddings. At D = 128 embedding dimensions and 3,200 training
frames, covariance estimation evidently costs more than shape information
buys; we did not sweep the shrinkage parameter, so the negative is specific
to this estimator configuration. **H6 is NOT supported** (2/5 seeds, mean
−0.027): energy (0.315 ± 0.063) and max-logit (0.302 ± 0.060) sit below
chance alongside MSP. All three output-space scores fail in the same way,
which locates the failure in the *space*, not in the particular confidence
functional. An internal consistency check: the CE+MSP per-seed AUROCs in
campaign 2 equal campaign 1's exactly, because the same seed produces the
same CE model — the pipeline is deterministic end to end.

### The auxiliary-outlier confound, measured (H7)

**H7 is supported in 5/5 seeds** (Figure 4, `fig4_confound.png`): rejection
of 4PAM unknowns is 0.404 ± 0.315 under the 8PAM-only outlier pool and
0.002 ± 0.004 under the 16PSK-only pool (paired per-seed difference +0.050
to +0.840). 64QAM rejection is low (0.06–0.08) in *every* arm. Campaign 1's
H2 gain is therefore almost entirely similarity transfer from the 8PAM
outliers to the 4PAM unknowns; outlier exposure did not generalize across
constellation families in this benchmark. Two further honest observations:
the PSK-only arm's overall AUROC (0.324 ± 0.081) is *below chance* — a
dissimilar outlier pool did not merely fail to help, and the 8PAM-only
arm's 4PAM rejection is wildly unstable across seeds (0.05 to 0.84,
sd 0.315), so even the similarity transfer is unreliable at this scale.

### SNR dependence of the MSP failure (H8)

**H8 is supported by the pre-registered criterion (4/5 seeds) but the
effect is small** (Figure 3, `fig3_snr_auroc.png`): CE+MSP AUROC is
0.380 ± 0.065 on frames at ≤ 2 dB and 0.338 ± 0.084 at ≥ 10 dB (mean paired
difference +0.042, one seed negative at −0.116). The direction matches the
confidence mechanism — the anti-correlation is strongest where the
classifier is most confident — but the per-SNR curve is not visually
monotone and the band difference is within one sd, so we treat H8 as weak
supporting evidence, not an established effect. The distance score stays
above 0.58 at every SNR.

**Interpretation.** The MSP failure mode is structural, not a training
artifact: 64QAM frames contain 16QAM-like level patterns and 4PAM is a
noisy BPSK superset, so the softmax assigns them to a known class with high
confidence; a confidence threshold then *accepts* them. The distance score
operates in embedding space, where the unknown constellations land away
from the known centroids even when the classifier is confident. Figure 6
shows the mechanism directly: in MSP space the unknown mass piles up at
score ≈ 0 — maximal confidence — on the *accept* side of the calibrated
threshold, while in distance space a large unknown mass sits far beyond it.
Campaign 2 sharpens the reading: every output-space functional we tried
(MSP, max-logit, energy) fails below chance on the same model, while both
embedding-space scores work, and the simpler of the two (Euclidean) works
best. This is consistent with feature-space detectors outperforming
output-space confidences under semantic similarity [5, 6], quantified here
under a fully controlled shift.

## 11. Limitations and Threats to Validity

- **Synthetic only.** Our transmitter model omits fading, multipath,
  filtering mismatch, timing jitter and real amplifier memory effects;
  nothing here predicts measured-RF performance.
- **Outlier-unknown similarity.** 8PAM (auxiliary) is close to 4PAM
  (unknown); campaign 2 measured this designed-in confound directly (H7):
  the Objectosphere gain on 4PAM collapses to 0.002 rejection when the
  similar outlier class is removed, and even with it the per-seed spread is
  large (sd 0.315). Campaign 1's H2 support should be read through this.
- **Mahalanobis configuration.** The H5 negative holds for one estimator
  (shared covariance, shrinkage 0.1); other shrinkage values, class-wise
  covariances or lower-dimensional embeddings were not swept and could
  reverse it.
- **H8 is weak evidence.** It passes the pre-registered sign criterion at
  4/5 seeds, but the band difference is within one sd and the per-SNR curve
  is not monotone.
- **Five seeds, no p-values.** The pre-registered sign-consistency criterion
  (≥ 4/5 seeds) bounds, not establishes, significance; 5/5 corresponds to a
  two-sided sign-test p of 0.0625.
- **Correlated frames.** Frames of one device are correlated; n counts
  frames. The seed is the unit of replication.
- **One architecture, one SNR grid, one calibration target.** We did not
  sweep ξ, λ, the 0.95 target, or architectures; conclusions are specific to
  this configuration.
- **Author degrees of freedom.** The unknown/auxiliary class assignment was
  fixed before the campaign but chosen by us; a different assignment could
  reverse H2.

## 12. Conclusion and Future Work

Under transmitter-level splits with explicit hardware impairments, every
output-space score we evaluated — MSP, max-logit, energy — failed below
chance on structurally related unknown modulations (paired on one model per
seed), while a simple embedding-distance score with the identical network
and calibration protocol reached AUROC 0.777 ± 0.015 and rejected half the
unknown frames at ~95% known acceptance. The principled upgrades did not
pay: Mahalanobis scoring trailed plain Euclidean distance in every seed
(H5), and the Objectosphere gain proved to be mostly similarity transfer
from the 8PAM outlier pool to the 4PAM unknowns (H7: 0.404 vs 0.002
rejection), unstable across seeds at that. Two pre-registered predictions
of ours were wrong and are reported as such (H4, H6/H5 directions). We release the
benchmark, pre-registration and per-seed results so the measurement can be
extended rather than trusted: natural next steps are a shrinkage/estimator
sweep for Mahalanobis [5], richer and genuinely disjoint outlier pools,
a channel model (fading/multipath), temperature-scaled confidences, and
validation against a measured dataset with per-device labels [10, 11].
